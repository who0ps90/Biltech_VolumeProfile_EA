# Known limitations and unverified behavior

1. **No broker-data or execution results.** Only compilation and portable synthetic specification tests have run. The native harness is compiled, not executed. Live/demo execution, feed coverage, realistic spread/costs, concurrency/crash recovery and actual partial fills remain unverified. No trading terminal/account was started and no profitability result is supplied.
2. **One terminal per account.** Portfolio locking coordinates cooperating charts in one running terminal. Its terminal-global mutex does not serialize another MT5 installation, VPS, machine, external EA or manual trader. Shared common-file persistence does not extend the live mutex across terminals. Use an isolated demo/research account with all group charts in one terminal.
3. **Native calls can block.** Tick/M1 loading occurs in small timer chunks, with bounded memory/retries and no history-loading call in `OnTick`. MT5 event handlers still share one EA thread, and native history/calendar/trade calls may wait internally (tick history can wait up to about 45 seconds). Group exposure pauses new profile loading but another chart could enter after that check. Protection and cutoff attempts can therefore be delayed. Hard real-time nonblocking operation would require a separately hosted data worker; this implementation does not promise it.
4. **Minute coverage is an observable proxy.** Having an event in every expected minute cannot prove that every intra-minute event was supplied. Broker APIs do not expose authoritative completeness/provenance for the full quote stream. The current published symbol schedule may differ historically; verified holiday overrides are required. A silent missing holiday or incorrect broker metadata cannot be inferred reliably.
5. **Synthetic tester ticks are not detectable reliably.** MT5 may generate/reconstruct unavailable or inconsistent periods. A minute-covered quote profile can still use synthetic ticks if the tester supplies them. Audit tester journals and broker data separately; exclude/label affected periods. The EA cannot certify a global exchange-volume source or a TradingView match.
6. **No unique trade-event identifiers.** Nonoverlapping chunk ranges and transactional retries prevent loader-created duplicates. Exact duplicate records already delivered by a broker cannot be distinguished from legitimate identical events in the same millisecond. Timestamp deduplication is deliberately avoided to preserve real event multiplicity.
7. **Conservative time support.** NY supports 1970–2099; EU broker rule supports 1996 onward. Explicit US broker transitions mean US UTC transition instants. Ambiguous/nonexistent NY/broker times and reference sessions crossing broker clock changes are rejected; configure a suitable reference or verified schedule. Historical broker policy must be supplied independently.
8. **Calendar authenticity.** CSV coverage is a provider assertion, not a proof. Synthetic fixtures are explicitly rejected by default. MT5 successful empty responses indicate no returned events, not independently certified release completeness. Tentative events block a whole server day; FOMC name/code classification and localization need inspection.
9. **Recovery can block deliberately.** Missing initial risk metadata, uncertain midnight equity, truncated opening history, unknown send outcomes or contaminated netting lifecycle block entries. Indeterminate requests have no unsafe timeout that grants permission to resubmit. Some require manual broker/history reconciliation. Fatal protection flags persist across NY days. Do not delete state with positions/orders outstanding.
10. **Baseline and costs.** Flat-midnight reconstruction relies on complete booked balance-impact history and standard broker accounting. Unbooked accrued credits/charges, separately posted commission without a position ID, corporate actions and unusual fee models may prevent exact attribution. The default commission estimate is zero until configured. Verify costs on the actual feed/account.
11. **Close certainty and broker protection.** SL/TP, market closes and TP adjustments may be rejected, delayed or filled at worse prices. Unknown close outcomes wait for evidence to avoid reversing a netting account. A netting position changed externally is left untouched even if that prevents automatic closure of the formerly owned portion. Open stops/targets are broker-side after acceptance, subject to broker operation.
12. **Sampling and audit.** Equity logs are EA-event sampled; official tester equity/report is the primary drawdown record for a dedicated test. Partial-deal Rs are contributions and need full-lifecycle recomputation. Logs after restart may lack metadata unavailable from history. Audit seen-deal globals can expire after four weeks, causing replayed audit rows; deduplicate by deal ID within the same run. Engine risk journals persist separately. Export/archive test-agent output before restarting or cleaning the agent.
13. **Implementation choices.** Setup states reset across NY days, references, entry-window closures and signal-history gaps. M1 data checks are stricter than relaxed tick coverage. Exact double accumulated-weight ties, tick-grid rounding and single-bin-at-a-time value-area expansion can differ from other platforms. These choices are documented and must be kept fixed in baseline research.
14. **Account history cost.** Execution reconciliation reads account lifecycles and can be expensive on accounts with very large histories. No native broker/account stress test has measured its latency. Use a dedicated research account and profile actual handler latency before forward use.

These limits are material. A clean compiler result and passing independent contracts do not establish that the deployed EA has passed an operational safety audit.
